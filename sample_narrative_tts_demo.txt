Demo Narrative Text for Text-to-Voice

This short passage is written for a voice demo. It contains narration, dialogue, pauses, and changes of rhythm, but it avoids unusual names and technical jargon.

At seven in the morning, the old station was almost empty. A thin line of sunlight crossed the floor and stopped at the edge of the ticket machine. Outside, the city was waking slowly: a bus sighed at the curb, a cyclist rang a bell, and somewhere behind the cafés a metal shutter rolled open with a sound like distant thunder.

Mara stood under the clock with a small blue suitcase. She had arrived early because she disliked rushing. She also disliked waiting, which made early arrival a complicated habit.

“You are always here before the story begins,” said Daniel, appearing beside her with two paper cups of coffee.

“That is not true,” Mara said. “Sometimes I arrive exactly when the story becomes inconvenient.”

He handed her a cup. “Then we are on schedule.”

The train was not due for twelve minutes. On the platform, an elderly man read the newspaper aloud to his dog, who seemed to disagree with every headline. A child in a yellow coat counted pigeons and lost track after nine. The loudspeaker crackled, took a deep electronic breath, and announced a delay in a voice that sounded polite but not sorry.

Mara smiled. “There it is. The inconvenience.”

Daniel looked at the departure board. “Twenty minutes. That is enough time to change your mind.”

“About the journey?”

“About everything.”

She considered this. The station smelled of coffee, cold iron, and wet stone. In the glass doors, she could see a reflection of the two of them: older than before, less certain, but still standing in the same frame.

“No,” she said at last. “Not today.”

The train arrived with a rush of air and a low metallic groan. Doors opened. People moved. The morning gathered itself into motion.

Mara lifted her suitcase. Daniel walked beside her. Neither of them said anything dramatic. That was better. Some departures are not endings. Some are simply the moment when waiting becomes movement.
